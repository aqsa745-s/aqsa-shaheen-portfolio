Optimization is the science of finding the best possible decision when resources are limited. It connects mathematics, statistics, data science and machine learning to practical problems faced by companies, hospitals and public organizations.

START WITH THE SIMPLE IDEA

What does optimization mean?

Imagine a hospital has a limited number of nurses, beds and operating rooms. It must decide how to schedule staff and allocate beds so patients receive timely care while costs and waiting times are controlled. Optimization compares possible plans and helps identify a strong plan that respects the hospital's limits.

01 / GOAL

Objective function

What are we trying to improve? For example, reduce delivery cost, waiting time or wasted stock.

02 / LIMITS

Constraints

What limits must we respect? Budget, staff hours, storage capacity, safety rules or available machines.

03 / CHOICE

Decision variables

What can we choose? How many units to order, how many staff to schedule or which route to use.

THE CONNECTION

How it connects to Data Science & ML.

Data science helps us understand what has happened. Machine learning can estimate what may happen next. Optimization uses those estimates, goals and constraints to recommend what to do.

01 / DATA SCIENCEUnderstandClean data, explore patterns and measure performance.
02 / MACHINE LEARNINGPredictEstimate demand, risk, patient flow or future workload.
03 / OPTIMIZATIONChooseFind a feasible plan that best meets the objective.
04 / DECISIONTake actionPresent recommendations for people to review.
Easy example: A model predicts a store may sell 120 bottles next week. An optimization model considers supplier lead time, storage space, budget and current stock to recommend an order quantity. Prediction estimates demand; optimization helps decide the order.

WHERE ORGANIZATIONS USE IT

Real-world applications.

RETAIL & E-COMMERCE

Inventory planning

Choose order quantities, reorder points, warehouse allocations and delivery routes to reduce excess stock, shortages and operating costs.

MEDICAL & HEALTHCARE

Hospital resource planning

Support nurse scheduling, operating-room timetables, bed allocation and ambulance positioning while respecting staffing and clinical constraints.

BANKING & FINANCE

Budget and risk

Allocate budgets, balance risk and return, schedule service capacity and prioritize work under policy and regulatory limits.

MANUFACTURING

Production planning

Decide production quantities and machine schedules while accounting for materials, capacity, deadlines and cost.

TRANSPORT

Routes and logistics

Plan vehicle routes and delivery schedules to reduce distance, fuel use and delays.

PUBLIC SERVICES

Resource allocation

Plan budgets, service locations and limited resources using transparent objectives and constraints.

Healthcare responsibility: Optimization is decision support, not a replacement for doctors or clinical judgment. Patient safety, fairness, privacy and medical rules must be essential constraints. Real deployments require expert validation.

MATHEMATICAL TOOLKIT

Methods you may come across.

METHODIN SIMPLE WORDSEXAMPLE
Linear ProgrammingFind the best result when relationships and constraints can be represented linearly.Allocate a limited budget across products.
Integer ProgrammingMake decisions that must be whole numbers.Schedule 8 nurses, not 8.4 nurses.
Mixed-Integer OptimizationCombine whole-number and continuous decisions.Choose which hospital units to open and how much capacity to assign.
Nonlinear OptimizationHandle objectives or constraints with nonlinear relationships.Complex cost or energy models.
Forecasting + OptimizationPredict future values, then use them to plan resources.Forecast medicine demand and plan replenishment.
